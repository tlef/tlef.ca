How many new frameworks have you learned over the last few years? NextJS, Svelte, Prisma, Vue, all of them? It feels like there's a new game-changer every month, and you start to wonder if you're falling behind.

This feeling has a name: Framework Fatigue, which occurs when the pace of new tools outstrips a reasonable person's ability to keep up. And if you're feeling it, take comfort in the fact that you're not alone. The web is full of developers venting about being overwhelmed by the endless stream of new frameworks and libraries.

## Regarding the Hype

Here's what the industry isn't telling you. **The most successful businesses are usually running on boring technology**, old, stable stacks that work. The majority of successful enterprises rely on established technologies, such as Java, PostgreSQL, or basic JavaScript, rather than the latest trends found on Hacker News.

_Think about it_: When you call a plumber, you don't ask if they're using the latest tool. You want your sink fixed. Good plumbers know which tools are most effective in getting the job done reliably. Great developers are the same.

## When and Why to Adopt Something New

The concern of framework fatigue doesn't mean you can't adopt new technology. Sometimes a new tool solves a problem that nothing else can, or becomes so popular that the ecosystem shifts. However, if you do decide to adopt a new tool, do it because it benefits your team or business, not your ego or resume. This is about empowering yourself with the right tools, not succumbing to the pressure of trends.

#### The Essential Developers look for:

- Stability and a track record of real-world use
- Clear business value, not just a novelty
- Great documentation and an active community
- Buy in from the team that needs to support, maintain, and hire for it

## The Cost of Chasing Trends

Every new framework comes with hidden costs:

- Time spent learning instead of building
- Team knowledge fragmentation (who knows what)
- Hiring difficulties, pulling from a tighter skill pool
- Technical debt from immature tooling

**Resume-driven development** is a real thing. Developers are picking technologies to pad their resumes and egos rather than solving business problems. Don't be that developer.

## Be Boring

The boring, essential developer is the one who keeps the team shipping, hiring, and scaling without the drama. You don't need to keep up with every new trend to be great at what you do.

Sometimes the most valuable move you can make is to choose what is proven, built to last, and boring.
